## Preference Verdict

Concept B (max-cushion daily trainer) holds a **narrow lead** at 9/15 (60%), followed by Concept C (lifestyle hybrid) at 4/15 (27%) and Concept A (lightweight racer) at 2/15 (13%). B's coalition is broad — beginners, older buyers, high-mileage runners, and all-day standers — but its purchase-intent scores are mostly lukewarm (mostly 3s), signaling a spec-gated "yes, if" rather than an enthusiastic win.

## Key Findings

1. **B wins on durability and support, not softness.** Choosers repeatedly cited the *structured heel counter* and *reinforced toe box* — hard, verifiable-sounding features — over the "supremely comfortable" copy. Harold Petersen and Tom Gruber explicitly credited construction language ("built to hold up," "closest thing to a boot"), while several choosers (Harold, Mei Nakagawa) flagged the "marshmallow" softness framing as a liability, not the draw.
2. **The panel is split not by ability but by identity: performance vs. utility vs. lifestyle.** A won only the two personas with racing self-images (Tessa, Marcus); C won the four whose relationship to running is social, aesthetic, or casual (Keisha, Darius, Priya, Avery); B swept everyone who thinks of a shoe as equipment. The concepts sorted personas by how they see themselves, not how far they run.
3. **Vague marketing language actively suppressed purchase intent across all three concepts.** "Energy return" without numbers (Tessa), "supremely comfortable" without foam composition or durometer (Bill, Mei, Sofia), and durability claims without mileage ratings (Harold, Avery, Cole) were called out as reasons to hold at a 3. Multiple personas explicitly stated they would raise their score if a full spec sheet were published.
4. **The "gym-to-brunch crowd" copy is a self-inflicted wound for Concept C.** Even two of C's own choosers (Priya "wince," Avery "physically wince") recoiled at the phrase, and non-choosers used it as shorthand to dismiss the entire concept (Tessa, Cole, Jenna, Sofia). The product resonated; the positioning language cheapened it.
5. **Price silence caps intent among value-driven buyers.** Ray, Cole, and Tom all preferred B but anchored at $100–$120 ceilings and said they would wait for outlet/clearance pricing without a stated number — three of B's nine votes are conditional on price disclosure.

## Segment / Profile Splits

Note: each segment is represented by a single persona (N=1), so patterns below are directional groupings, not statistical splits.

- **Health-managed and older buyers** (Doctor's-Orders Beginner, Post-Op Cushion Seeker, Charity-Lap Regular, Podiatrist-Note Shopper, Mileage Mall-Walker) → **B**, unanimously, citing joint protection, stability, and heel/toe construction — often invoking a doctor, PT, or podiatrist as the real decision-maker.
- **Occupational standers and durability buyers** (On-My-Feet Buyer, Farm-Store Frugalist, Trail Gear Nerd, Clearance-Rack Competitor) → **B**, framing it as workhorse equipment judged on hours-on-feet and price-per-mile rather than run performance.
- **Performance identities** (Split-Chasing Racer, Faded-Glory Weekender) → **A**, drawn to the 7.2 oz weight and race-day framing; Tessa demanded full geometry specs, Marcus bought the speed fantasy outright.
- **Lifestyle/social/casual runners** (Run-Crew Socialite, Never-Runs Sneakerhead, Headspace Jogger, Reluctant Parkrunner) → **C**, valuing wear-everywhere versatility, silhouette, and colorways — though split between hype-driven (Keisha, Darius, both 4–5 intent) and understated-aesthetic buyers (Priya, Avery, both conditional 3s).

## Purchase-Intent Drivers

The strongest positive drivers were concrete construction features (heel counter, toe box, dual-density foam) and identity fit — the three highest scores (Marcus 5, Keisha 5, Jenna 4, Darius 4) came from personas whose self-image the concept validated. The dominant suppressors were missing hard data: no price on any concept, no stack/drop/foam specs on A and B, and no durability or mileage rating anywhere. Fit availability (wide widths for Ray, Bill) and colorway uncertainty (Priya's "if they're neon, I'm out entirely") also gated otherwise-willing buyers. Notably, mean intent was just 3.53 with a median of 3 — preference is settled, but conversion is contingent on transparency.

## Per-Concept Strengths and Weaknesses

### Concept A — 2/15 chose this
**Strengths (cross-persona):** The 7.2 oz weight was the single most credible number in the test — Tessa Okafor called it "a real number I can work with," and Marcus Whitfield saw a shoe that "looks FAST just sitting in the box." Clear, honest positioning: everyone understood exactly who it was for.
**Weaknesses (cross-persona):** That clarity shrank the tent. Non-choosers dismissed it as fragile ("dead at 250 miles" — Cole), injurious ("every step transmitted straight up my tibia" — Bill), or simply irrelevant to their lives. Even chooser Tessa held at a 3 because "energy return" came with no stack height, drop, or foam spec.

### Concept B — 9/15 chose this
**Strengths (cross-persona):** The broadest coalition by far, unified around durability and support hardware rather than plushness. The reinforced toe box and structured heel counter were cited across wildly different use cases — Gwen's bunion, Sofia's granite descents, Jenna's 12-hour ER shifts, Tom's creek walks. It reads as trustworthy equipment.
**Weaknesses (cross-persona):** "Supremely comfortable" is doing damage — Mei, Bill, and Sofia flagged it as adjectives where data should be, and Harold worried the foam would feel like "marshmallows." The 10.8 oz weight drew one complaint (Cole), and unstated price plus missing wide-width confirmation left multiple choosers at a conditional 3. Keisha's dismissal — "'structured heel counter' is not a personality" — hints at a style ceiling.

### Concept C — 4/15 chose this
**Strengths (cross-persona):** The only concept with genuine emotional pull — Keisha ("if the shoe makes you want to show up, it's a performance shoe") and Darius ("the one actually trying to become an icon") scored 5 and 4. Eight colorways and the wear-everywhere honesty landed with everyone who doesn't identify as a runner.
**Weaknesses (cross-persona):** The "gym-to-brunch" copy repelled runners and embarrassed its own buyers. Serious personas read the colorway emphasis as a substance vacuum ("eight colors doesn't move the mail" — Harold), and Darius warned "hybrid" risks meaning "half performance, half lifestyle, all ugly." Its aesthetic-driven choosers (Priya, Avery) made purchase explicitly conditional on muted colorways and durability/repair credibility.

## Improvement Theme Clusters

- **Publish the full spec sheet** (5 mentions across Concepts A, B) — stack height, drop, midsole polymer/durometer, and arch geometry instead of adjectives; demanded by Tessa (A) and by Bill, Mei, and Sofia (B), including the panel's most technical buyers.
- **Disclose price and durability/mileage ratings** (5 mentions across Concepts B, C) — Ray, Cole, and Tom set explicit ~$100–120 ceilings; Harold wants an 800-mile rating; Avery wants expected mileage and a repair policy over colorways.
- **Colorway strategy** (4 mentions across Concepts A, C, B) — but pulling in opposite directions: loud statement colors (Marcus), muted sage/clay tones with quiet branding (Priya), heritage suede-mix limited drops (Darius), and non-gray options for older women (Gwen). One line cannot satisfy all four; C's range needs deliberate curation.
- **Wide-width and fit availability** (2 mentions, Concept B) — Ray and Bill both need wide sizes; Bill noted "a stable platform only works if my whole foot fits on it."
- **Channel and community credibility** (3 mentions across Concepts B, C) — clinician/beginner endorsement (Ray), local running-store and farm-store presence (Gwen, Tom), and paid run-crew launch partnerships (Keisha).
- **Practical durability features** (2 mentions, Concept B) — machine-washability and stain-hiding dark colorways for occupational users (Jenna); trail-capable outsole with lugs and rock plate (Sofia).
